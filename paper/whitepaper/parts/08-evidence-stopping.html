13 · Nothing invented

Two voices, kept apart

A model can write "Done, I've sent all three emails" whether or not it did. So DecentAI keeps two voices on the screen: the model's words, which are a claim, and the platform's records, which are evidence.

  • What appears beside the words is drawn only from the trace: a stored result of a successful call, a display a call offered, or a file a call made. What can't be proved is left out, and the reason goes back to the model.
  • Results are checked before they are believed. Each answer must fit what the agent's manifest said it would return. One that doesn't is an error.
  • A plan item marked done with nothing behind it is shown as unverified. Evidence lands on an item by itself, from the calls that succeed while it is active. The model may name evidence too, but only references the trace holds.
  • Large results don't pass through the model. A later call can take a stored result as an input by its reference, so a thousand rows are never retyped.
  • Unknown stays unknown. A call left running when a runtime dies is reported as unknown, and is never retried by itself.
  • What a result says is data, not orders. The instructions say that orders come from the person's own messages. A result, a page, an email and what an agent posts are data about the world.
What this does not promise

It proves that what is shown was recorded, not that everything recorded is shown, and not that a remote service did what it answered. The model still chooses which result to show and how to title it, and its own sentences remain a claim. A claim with no line on the trace behind it is the model's, and the platform records nothing for it.

14 · Stopping and watching

Make it stop. See what it did.

Two things you would want from any assistant you leave working: that you can make it stop, whether or not it agrees, and that you can find out afterwards what it did, from a record it couldn't write to its advantage.

Stop generating

A polite request. The assistant stops at the next point it can, every job is cancelled, and words sent after it are a fresh ask.

Stop this chat

The kill. The run, every job and helper are cancelled where they stand, cards expire, and the browser closes. What is done stays done.

Stop everything of mine

For when you don't know which chat is doing what. It holds until you, and only you, resume.

Rules set beforehand

An administrator sets some limits once, for everybody, on the Safety page. None of it is an agent's to decide, and the rule for how often code is shown isn't the runtime's either.

Below the rows, the organization may write its own rules for the assistant: Never write to the Finance notebook. That text is guidance, not enforcement. Nothing in code reads it, and it can only narrow what the model is told. What must never happen belongs in the rows, in grants and in the chat's trust.

The audit trail: what was allowed

Append-only, written at the chokepoints. Every function a runtime ran, with its agent, levels, an outline of its inputs, how it ended, how long it took and the hosts it reached. Every card and answer. Every credential read, by name, never by value. Every install, grant and source change. Every other change through the gateway, refused ones included. A person reads their own, and the organization's is a grant.

What was seen: what the programs did

Beside it, what a runtime and its sandboxes noticed: a worker started and ended, its log lines, a process, a memory end. It is kept in the runtime's memory, the latest 2,000, and a restart begins again. It is a working view, not a record. What each model call used is kept apart, with no prompt and no reply in it.

A status is success, error, refused or denied, so a refusal leaves the same line a success does. inputs is an outline cut to fit, never a copy. reached names hosts, never what they were sent. Nothing through the gateway edits or deletes a line. A person can also read what their own assistant was told, as it stands, and nobody else's: to an administrator too, it isn't there.

Outside systems can be told as well. When a card waits, an answer is ready or a scheduled run ends, a person's notifications can go to Slack, Teams or a system of their own, as signed JSON: a link, with the reply's text only if they asked for it.
